Censorship Reference

Banned Works of the World

A reference catalogue of notable censorship cases: books banned, burned, or otherwise suppressed by governments, churches, and other authorities across history. (This is a research / reading list; BABE'S BOOKSTORE carries only public-domain / openly-licensed works.)

We keep this list because a bookstore is only honest if it refuses to flinch. Suppression is not a moral judgement — it is a fact about power; the same book that is an authority’s scandal is a reader’s freedom. Where a title is public domain, the name links into our catalogue so you can read the very editions that were once taken off shelves. In-print titles are listed for reference and are not sold here.

Below, each entry records the title, the author, and the year of its suppression. These are the documented tipping points of library history — from the 1885 Concord, Massachusetts police ban of Huckleberry Finn to the 1989 Satanic Verses fatwa. The catalogue links point at the public-domain editions we ourselves carry.

Europe — church, crown & state
  • Ovid — The Art of Love — exiled by Augustus, 8 CE
  • François Rabelais — Pantagruel — condemned by the Sorbonne, 1533
  • Thomas More — Utopia — placed on the Index of Forbidden Books, 1559
  • Galileo Galilei — Dialogue Concerning the Two Chief World Systems — banned 1633, under house arrest for the rest of his life
  • John Cleland — Fanny Hill — banned/obscene in England for roughly two centuries
  • Denis Diderot — Encyclopédie — license revoked by the French crown, 1759
  • Marquis de Sade — Justine — Napoleon ordered his detention and the seizure of his papers, 1801
  • Gustave Flaubert — Madame Bovary — French obscenity trial, 1857 (acquitted)
  • Charles Baudelaire — Les Fleurs du Mal — French conviction, 1857; six poems suppressed until 1949
  • Henrik Ibsen — Ghosts — refused stage production across Europe, 1881–1891
  • Thomas Hardy — Jude the Obscure — burned by the Bishop of Wakefield, 1895
  • Leo Tolstoy — Resurrection — censored in Russia, 1899
  • James Joyce — Ulysses — serial installments seized in the US, 1920; UK publication suppressed until 1933
  • D. H. Lawrence — The Rainbow — seized under obscenity law in the UK, 1915; 1,000+ copies destroyed
  • D. H. Lawrence — Lady Chatterley’s Lover — publisher Vivian Greenwood’s 1929 pirate edition prosecuted; UK clean run until R v Penguin Books, 1960
  • Aldous Huxley — Brave New World — banned in Ireland, 1932
  • Erich Maria Remarque — All Quiet on the Western Front — burned by the Nazis, 1933
  • Heinrich Mann, Thomas Mann, Bertolt Brecht, Stefan Zweig, Ernst Toller, Magnus Hirschfeld’s Institute of Sexology — 1933 Nazi book burnings
  • George Orwell — Animal Farm & Nineteen Eighty-Four — suppressed across the Soviet bloc
  • Boris Pasternak — Doctor Zhivago — banned in the USSR, 1958
  • Aleksandr Solzhenitsyn — One Day in the Life of Ivan Denisovich — withdrawn from Soviet circulation, 1965
  • Aleksandr Solzhenitsyn — The Gulag Archipelago — published in samizdat; banned in the USSR, 1973
Africa — colonial & post-colonial bans
  • Jomo Kenyatta — Facing Mount Kenya — banned in colonial Kenya, 1950s
  • Ngũgĩ wa Thiong’o — A Grain of Wheat & Petals of Blood — banned in Kenya, 1977
  • Ngũgĩ wa Thiong’o — Weep Not, Child & The River Between — withdrawn from Kenya’s school syllabus during the Kamirithu years
  • Steve Biko — I Write What I Like — banned under apartheid South Africa
  • Nelson Mandela — No Easy Walk to Freedom — banned under apartheid South Africa
  • André Brink — Kennis van die aand (Looking on Darkness) — banned in South Africa, 1974
  • Wole Soyinka — The Man Died — banned in Nigeria, 1972
  • Frantz Fanon — The Wretched of the Earth — restricted under apartheid South Africa
  • Hundreds of African National Congress titles — banned under apartheid, c. 1963 onward
  • Tsitsi Dangarembga — This Mournable Body — her publisher pulled the novel amid state pressure in Zimbabwe, 2021
Americas — obscenity trials & school challenges
  • Harriet Beecher Stowe — Uncle Tom’s Cabin — banned across the antebellum South, 1850s
  • Frederick Douglass — Narrative — banned in Southern states; enslaved people faced punishment for learning to read
  • Walt Whitman — Leaves of Grass — Whitman fired from the Interior Department over it, 1865
  • Mark Twain — Adventures of Huckleberry Finn — first ban by the Concord, Massachusetts public library, 1885
  • James Joyce — Ulysses — United States v. One Book Called Ulysses, 1933; Judge Woolsey’s ruling opened the door to modern literature
  • John Steinbeck — The Grapes of Wrath — burned in Kern County, California, 1939
  • William Faulkner — Sanctuary — suppressed in Cambridge, Massachusetts, 1931
  • Henry Miller — Tropic of Cancer — obscenity cases in US states through the 1960s; cleared by the Supreme Court, 1964
  • Allen Ginsberg — Howl — obscenity trial, San Francisco, 1957 (acquitted)
  • William S. Burroughs — Naked Lunch — Massachusetts Supreme Court decision, 1966
  • D. H. Lawrence — Lady Chatterley’s Lover — Grove Press v. Christenberry, New York, 1959
  • J. D. Salinger — The Catcher in the Rye — among the most challenged American novels of the late 20th century
  • Kurt Vonnegut — Slaughterhouse-Five — burned and challenged in US schools, 1970s–80s
  • Ray Bradbury — Fahrenheit 451 — ironically banned and challenged in US schools after 1976
  • Toni Morrison — Beloved — challenged for sexual content, 1990s onward
  • Bret Easton Ellis — American Psycho — challenged in US libraries and schools, 1990s
  • Harper Lee — To Kill a Mockingbird — challenged and removed from school curricula, 1960s onward
Asia & the Middle East — religious & state bans
  • Khalil Gibran — The Prophet — banned in Lebanon, and later acclaimed worldwide
  • Vladimir Nabokov — Lolita — published in Paris, 1955, after five US refusals; banned in the UK, France and New Zealand before its US release
  • Salman Rushdie — Midnight’s Children — briefly subject to legal challenge in India, 1981
  • Salman Rushdie — Shame — banned in Pakistan, 1984
  • Salman Rushdie — The Satanic Verses — burned in England and India, 1988; fatwa issued 1989; the author lived under a price on his head for a decade
  • North Korea — a total national ban on nearly all foreign books
Alongside the canon
  • Percy Bysshe Shelley — Queen Mab — publisher Thomas William Hodgskin imprisoned for seditious libel, 1821
  • Ambrose Bierce — The Devil’s Dictionary — banned in several US jurisdictions for irreverence
  • Émile Zola — Germinal — prosecuted and withdrawn in Russia, censored elsewhere
  • Radclyffe Hall — The Well of Loneliness — UK obscenity trial, 1928; withdrawn
  • James Baldwin — banned and rejected across US libraries in the 1960s for candour about race and sexuality
  • The banned-books lists of the American Library Association and Anne Haight’s Banned Books — hundreds of further titles catalogued by decades

The ALA and Anne Haight catalogues are browsable in the library — open the ALA Ban List or Anne Haight’s list, in the African Literature section where the first crucible was often a colonial office.
